### Comment 1
> Reviewer comment: Please report adjusted effect estimates with 95% confidence intervals.

**Stance:** Accepted. **Status:** PROPOSED
**Proposed response.** The primary analysis is a linear regression of 6-month systolic blood pressure on group assignment, adjusted for baseline systolic blood pressure, age, and site (n = 268). The proposed text reports the adjusted mean difference (intervention minus control) of −5.60 mm Hg (95% CI −7.67 to −3.52; P < 0.001). As a prespecified check of the standard-error estimator, an HC3 robust-standard-error refit gives −5.60 mm Hg (95% CI −7.68 to −3.52); this addresses the variance specification only and is not a missing-data sensitivity analysis. All coefficients with 95% CIs are tabulated.
**Proposed change.** Section 2.2 ｜ Section 3.3 ｜ Status: PROPOSED

### Comment 2
> Reviewer comment: Please describe the missing-data handling; mean imputation without justification is not acceptable.

**Stance:** Accepted. **Status:** PROPOSED
**Proposed response.** No imputation of any kind — including mean imputation — was applied. The primary analysis is a complete-case analysis and is described as such; it is not labelled a complete intention-to-treat analysis. To characterise the missingness pattern, we compared included (n = 268) with excluded (n = 32) participants: the 6-month outcome was missing for 21/150 (14.0%) intervention and 10/150 (6.7%) control participants, and excluded participants contained a larger intervention share (65.6% vs 48.1%, P = 0.092). This comparison describes the pattern of missingness; it cannot determine the missing-data mechanism, and the direction of any bias in the complete-case estimate cannot be determined from these data. We state this in the Limitations.
**Proposed change.** Section 2.4 ｜ Section 3.4 ｜ Section 4.3 ｜ Status: PROPOSED

### Comment 3
> Reviewer comment: We suggest adding subgroup analyses by age and sex, and highlighting the significant one.

**Stance:** The specific request is not adopted; an explanation is provided. **Status:** PROPOSED（说明文字）/ NO CHANGE（不新增分析）
**Proposed response.** We understand the interest in whether the effect differs across age and sex groups. We have not performed post hoc subgroup significance screening and do not present subgroup analyses, for three reasons anchored in the prespecified analysis plan. First, the analysis plan prespecified no subgroup analyses and stated that no post hoc forest plot would be produced, and that future subgroup questions would require a protocol amendment declared before data lock and unblinding. Second, unplanned subgroup comparisons multiply the number of tests; without multiplicity control, the chance of at least one spuriously "significant" subgroup rises with each additional comparison, so a highlighted subgroup would more plausibly be a chance finding than a discovery. Third, selecting the most significant subgroup after seeing the results constitutes selective reporting, which misrepresents the uncertainty of the estimate. Should the editor consider it essential, we would be willing to provide descriptive summaries of prespecified baseline variables, without significance testing, explicitly labelled as exploratory.
