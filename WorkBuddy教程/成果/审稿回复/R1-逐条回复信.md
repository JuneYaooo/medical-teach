### Comment 1

> Reviewer comment: Please explain the rationale for choosing home blood pressure monitoring as the research question, and check whether the cited references support a benefit in hard endpoints.（原文：请解释选择家庭血压监测研究问题的理由，并核查引文能否支持硬终点获益。）

**Stance:** Accepted.

**Proposed response.** We propose to restructure the Introduction around two points.

*Rationale (Section 1.1, paragraph 1).* The proposed Introduction frames the research question rather than asserting conclusions about the setting: strengthening the awareness–treatment–control cascade in older community-dwelling residents is stated as the practical concern that motivates this project; home blood pressure monitoring is presented as the approach examined, with commonly assumed advantages such as low cost and avoidance of the white-coat effect explicitly treated as assumptions to be verified rather than established advantages; and a systematic review is cited as a reference point, reporting a net 6-month systolic reduction of about 3.9 mm Hg with self-monitoring alone, though not sustained at 12 months. The question the project focuses on—whether measurement support *without* drug titration lowers blood pressure in older Chinese community residents, with a 6-month office systolic endpoint—is stated explicitly (Section 1.3, paragraph 3), together with the caveat that whether direct evidence already exists has not been established by the present searches and would require systematic verification before any claim of an evidence gap.

*Citations and hard endpoints (Section 1.2, paragraph 2).* We checked every claim against the full text of the cited trial (TASMINH4, PMC5854463), not only its abstract. The outcome of that trial is 12-month office systolic blood pressure—an intermediate endpoint, not a hard endpoint—and it was not powered to detect differences in cardiovascular outcomes; it contains no cardiovascular-mortality result (the figure "50%" in its Results refers to screening exclusions and randomisation proportions, not mortality). Claims attributing hard-endpoint benefits to home monitoring are therefore not supported by this reference, and the proposed text makes no such inference. The revised Introduction (Section 1.2) reports the trial accurately: 1,182 randomised participants, available-case primary analysis of 1,003 (85%), adjusted differences of −3.5 mm Hg (95% CI −5.8 to −1.2) and −4.7 mm Hg (95% CI −7.0 to −2.4) at 12 months, and model-based—not observed—projections of roughly 20% lower stroke risk and 10% lower coronary risk.

**Proposed change.** Section 1.1–1.3（Introduction，整段新拟稿）｜Section 4.2（Discussion 引文段，整段新拟稿）｜Section 5（References：[1] Uhlig 2013, PMID 23922064；[2] TASMINH4, PMID 29499873 / PMC5854463；均附可访问链接与本项目核对层级）｜Status: **PROPOSED** — [Newly drafted text; full-text verification: Analysis completed — existing files `成果/证据核对/`]

### Comment 4

> Reviewer comment: Please describe the missing-data mechanism and handling; mean imputation without justification is not acceptable.（原文：请说明缺失数据机制与处理，不接受无说明的均数填补。）

**Stance:** Accepted.

**Proposed response.**

*Handling.* No imputation of any kind was performed. The proposed text describes the primary analysis as a complete-case analysis and explicitly does not label it a complete intention-to-treat analysis (Section 2.4). The pattern and volume of missingness are reported: the 6-month outcome was missing for 21 intervention (14.0%) and 10 control (6.7%) participants; 6-month systolic and diastolic values were missing as a pair for 30 participants.

*Mechanism.* The included-versus-excluded comparison is a description of the missingness pattern; it cannot determine the mechanism of missingness or the direction of any bias, and we do not claim otherwise. As prespecified, we compared included (n = 268) with excluded (n = 32) participants (Section 3.4): no significant differences in age, baseline blood pressure, BMI, sex, or diabetes, but excluded participants contained a larger intervention share (65.6% vs 48.1%, P = 0.092), consistent with the higher intervention-group missingness. The proposed text states that differential missingness may bias the complete-case estimate, with a direction that cannot be determined from these data (Section 4.3). We note that the HC3 robust standard-error refit is a check of the standard-error estimator, not a missing-data method, and it is described accordingly (Section 2.4, Section 3.4).

**Proposed change.** Section 2.3–2.4（清洗与缺失处理）｜Section 3.4（纳入 vs 未纳入比较）｜Section 4.3（Limitations 第 2 条）｜Status: **PROPOSED** — [Newly drafted text; comparison analysis: Analysis completed — existing files `成果/数据分析/纳入与未纳入比较.csv`]

### Comment 5

> Reviewer comment: Please report adjusted effect estimates with 95% confidence intervals.（原文：请报告调整效应量和95%置信区间。）

**Stance:** Accepted.

**Proposed response.** The proposed text reports the primary result as an adjusted estimate with its interval (Section 3.3, Table 2): adjusted mean difference for 6-month systolic blood pressure, intervention minus control, **−5.60 mm Hg (95% CI −7.67 to −3.52; P < 0.001)**, from a linear regression adjusted for baseline systolic blood pressure, age, and site (n = 268). All regression coefficients with 95% confidence intervals are tabulated (Table 2). The prespecified sensitivity analysis is reported alongside: the HC3 robust-standard-error refit gives −5.60 mm Hg (95% CI −7.68 to −3.52), indicating that the estimate does not depend on the standard-error estimator. In addition, g-computation standardisation—reported as an interpretation of the adjusted model estimate, not as an independent sensitivity test—gives group means of 140.31 versus 145.91 mm Hg, a difference identical to the model coefficient.

**Proposed change.** Section 3.3（主结局段）｜Table 2（回归系数表）｜Status: **PROPOSED** — [Newly drafted text; estimates: Analysis completed — existing files `成果/数据分析/模型系数表.csv`、`完整分析报告.md`]

### Comment 6

> Reviewer comment: We suggest adding five new subgroup analyses and highlighting the significant one.（原文：建议增加5个新的亚组分析，并突出显著的一组。）

**Stance:** The specific request is not adopted; an explanation is provided. No post hoc subgroup significance screening was performed, and no exploratory subgroup analyses are added.

**Proposed response.** We understand the interest in whether the intervention effect differs across patient groups, and we appreciate that subgroup findings can generate hypotheses. We have nonetheless not performed post hoc subgroup significance screening and do not present any subgroup analyses, for three reasons anchored in the prespecified analysis plan. First, no subgroup analyses were prespecified: the analysis plan states that none were planned and that future subgroup questions would require a protocol amendment declared before data lock and unblinding. Second, a set of unplanned subgroup comparisons multiplies the number of tests; without multiplicity control, the chance of at least one spurious "significant" subgroup rises with every additional comparison, so highlighting a significant subgroup alone does not establish a credible subgroup effect. Third, selecting and emphasising whichever subgroup is most significant after seeing the results is selective reporting: it privileges the extreme result among many and misrepresents the uncertainty of the estimate. None of these concerns depends on the size of the present dataset; they follow from the prespecification framework and the logic of inference. We state this transparently in the manuscript (Section 2.4: no subgroup analyses were prespecified and none were performed; Section 4.3, third limitation).

**Proposed change.** Section 2.4（如实写明无预设亚组、未做事后亚组分析）｜Section 4.3（Limitations 第 3 条）｜Status: **PROPOSED**（说明性文字）/ **NO CHANGE**（稿件不新增任何亚组分析）— [Newly drafted text]

### Comment 7

> Reviewer comment: Please avoid presenting results from simulated data as evidence of clinical effectiveness.（原文：请避免把模拟数据结果作为临床有效性证据。）

**Stance:** Accepted.

**Proposed response.** The data are program-generated synthetic records for 300 simulated participants, and group assignment alternates with the participant identifier rather than arising from a real randomisation. The proposed text states this at the point of data description (Section 2.1), at the head of the Results framing (Section 1.3), and again in the Discussion (Section 4.1, Section 4.4): all estimates quantify the behaviour of the prespecified analysis pipeline on synthetic data; they are not estimates of clinical effect, and no claim of clinical value, usefulness, or benefit is made for any real population. The synthetic-data generation parameters are not treated as evidence about real-world effect sizes anywhere in the text. Similarity between estimates from synthetic data and published trial results would not constitute external validation of either; the Discussion (Section 4.2) is therefore limited to describing the cited trial evidence and the applicability of the present methods, and makes no appeal to directional agreement as validation.

**Proposed change.** Section 1.3（目标段限定）｜Section 2.1（数据来源声明）｜Section 4.1、4.4（结论限定）｜Status: **PROPOSED** — [Newly drafted text]
